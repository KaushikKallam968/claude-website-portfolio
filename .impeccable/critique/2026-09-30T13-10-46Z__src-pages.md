## Design Specificity Verdict

LLM assessment: the concept is authored for this person and could not be moved to another portfolio. The page keeps notes on its reader and says what each note cannot tell, which is the Instrumentation case's coverage-versus-quality argument made live; the lens tally and the closing readout of the visitor's own time are a quantitative researcher's moves. The visual language is less specific: Switzer on paper, hairlines, mono metadata and a big clock is the current Swiss-editorial portfolio register, and a dotted world map with a blue arc is a common "global" motif. The live terminator, real local clocks and the dot-formed name keep it on the right side of the line. Biggest missed opportunity: the only chart on a Quantitative UX Researcher's site measures the visitor, not the work.

Deterministic scan: source scan (src/pages, src/components, src/layouts) exit 0, 0 findings, also 0 with --no-config. ## Overall Impression

A portfolio with a real idea and the craft to carry it: the observation layer argues research judgment instead of claiming it, and the details (Return, reduced motion, no-JS) hold. It loses points on the busiest reader's path: Contact lands on a chart of zeros, the flight scenes eat most of the scroll, and the map lingers under real text. Single biggest opportunity: make the recruiter's path (identity, one case, contact) as authored as the journey.

## What's Working

1. The observation layer is the argument, not decoration: "can't tell" lines, the lens tally, and the Instrumentation case pointing back at the margin show judgment directly.
2. Case structure built for a research peer: question, Contribution/Timeline/Methods/Outcome type, a figure captioned with what it is not, prose, "What the evidence shows, and what it can't", Status. Mexico's scoping decision is exactly the evidence a hiring manager looks for.
3. Details hold: Return restores exact scroll and focus with a "Back to New York" label; reduced motion, no-JS and dark mode are each complete pages; the résumé is a clean recruiter document with Save as PDF.
