## Localization was more than language

Chegg needed to understand Mexican students’ academic experiences as it expanded into the market. Localizing a learning product meant understanding how students studied, the support they relied on, and how they found learning resources, not only translating the interface.

## Choosing the approach

The study began with a comparative survey of 1,000 students, split evenly between the US and Mexico, covering academic challenges, emotional experiences, study habits and use of support services. We recruited through Dynata, which also handled the Spanish translation, and ran the survey in Forsta. The comparison established broad patterns and told the qualitative phase where to look.

I then ran unmoderated interviews with 12 English-speaking college students studying in Mexico, recruited through UserTesting. Time constraints shaped that choice. The interviews examined students’ context and how they used learning resources in more depth than a survey could.

## A consequential scoping decision

The international team had many questions, and product and design stakeholders wanted to investigate different areas. The first survey draft ran past 100 questions.

I explained survey fatigue and cognitive overload and helped the team prioritize. The real question was what the study needed to answer within a questionnaire students could realistically finish. The final focus areas covered academic support, standardized tests, study habits and the emotional experience of studying.

## Findings and product direction

The research identified needs around major projects, standardized-test preparation and academic support, which pointed to relevant practice resources and support timed to students’ routines.

The interviews also showed students moving between Spanish and English when they looked for help. Their search behavior crossed a language boundary, which mattered for a content database that was largely in English. I recommended that a question searched in Spanish should be able to retrieve a relevant answer from Chegg’s English database. My presentation notes record that the feature was implemented on chegg.mx.
