September 30, 2026. Primary captures of live sites (desktop 1440×900 at five scroll positions, a six-frame strip of the first scroll, and a 390×844 phone view) are in `docs/research/teardown/<site>/`, with a contact sheet per site in `sheet.jpg`. ## Characteristics that recur

1. **The subject's real material carries the page.** Their photographs, their work, their writing, their hand. Nothing is generic stock or generated.
2. **Typography is architecture.** The name is set as a graphic at extreme scale; a second voice (mono, serif or handwriting) annotates. Scale contrast, not decoration.
3. **An index, not a grid of cards.** Numbered lists, tables of contents, dossiers. Structure reads as authored.
4. **One signature interaction, perfected.** Aristide's slice strip, Robin's bent planes, Rauno's sideways words, Dennis's marquee. Everything else stays quiet.
5. **Narrow palette.** Usually two colours; imagery supplies the rest.
6. **True, specific details.** "v.XIX", "Available October 2026", a live local clock, "Located in the Netherlands". Things only that person could write.
7. **The site does what the person does.** Bruno builds 3D, so the site is a 3D world. Josh teaches, so it is a teaching site. Lynn makes CSS art, so the site is CSS art.

## What this means for Kaushik

Kaushik studies behavior and is careful about what data can and cannot explain. Following characteristic 7, the site should do that too: quietly observe how it is being read and say, honestly, what each observation cannot tell. Following 1 and 6, its material is his real words, real places (true coordinates and live local time), real watched. screens from the public App Store listing, and diagrams of his actual research ideas rather than stock or generated scenery. Following 2 to 5, the name is the hero at full width, a mono annotation voice carries the observations, the work is a numbered index, and the palette is paper, ink and one annotation blue.
